What the product has already decided, and you must not relitigate
From the backend's flows document and ADR 0012. These are closed; if you believe one is wrong, say so and stop, as `CLAUDE.md` requires.
Require availability and at least one goal. Nothing more. Closed decision F2, with the reason: without those two the optimiser has nothing to work with, and a default plan would be "ficção apresentada como recomendação". So setup asks for exactly two things, and the screen must not grow a third.
The student declares no effort estimate. Closed decision L1: effort comes from the curated `topic.effortTier`. Do not ask how long a topic takes, how hard it is, or how well they know it.
A goal is a whole subject, never a selection of topics. Closed decision L3: every topic of the subject enters, with no exclusions in v1, and `GoalRequest` takes a `subjectId`. The topic list is context for an informed choice, not a set of checkboxes. Make that visually unambiguous.
The target date is a prioritisation constraint, not a horizon. The operation says so, and the plan horizon is fixed at four weeks (closed decision F3). A student who sets a date eight months out gets a four-week plan. The screen must not imply otherwise — no "your plan will cover until…", no countdown to the date, no progress bar toward it. This is the one place in this task where a well-meaning interface would lie to a student.
Facts read from the contract — verify each, and report anything different
`GET /api/v1/subjects` — "Subjects have no curated order, so position is their rank in catalogue-code order, starting at 1." So `position` is not a ranking and must not be shown as one. `GET /api/v1/subjects/{subjectId}/topics` is in curricular order, and is also where a topic name is resolved for a client holding only an identifier (which the agenda will need later).
`GET /api/v1/availability?on=<yyyy-MM-dd>`. The date is a query parameter and is deliberately not defaulted to today: "these are local times in the account's own zone and the server does not decide what day it is for somebody else." With no `on`, you get every window ever declared, closed ones included. So compute the date in the account's zone through `AccountTimeService` and pass it — never let the browser's zone decide, and never omit it and filter on the client.
`POST /api/v1/availability` — `dayOfWeek`, `effectiveFrom`, `startTime`, `endTime` required, `effectiveUntil` optional. Times are local, with no offset, `HH:mm` in the snapshot's examples. Errors: 400 "The window closes before it opens, or stops applying before it applied" — both preventable in the form, so prevent them and still handle the answer; 409 "It would overlap an existing window", with the reason worth telling the student: overlapping windows would make whatever allocates study hours count the same hour twice; 403 "This account's learning data may not be processed", which the gate chain should make unreachable but does not make impossible.
F19 is still open and bites here: availability echoes `startTime`/`endTime` as `HH:mm:ss` (`"19:00:00"`) while the contract's examples and the design system say `HH:mm`. Accept both on the way in, send `HH:mm` on the way out, and put the normalisation in one place.
`POST /api/v1/goals` — `subjectId` required; `priority` an integer 1–5 with a default of 3; `targetDate` optional. 409 "There is already an active goal for that subject". `PATCH /api/v1/goals/{goalId}` requires `priority` and takes an optional `targetDate`; the subject cannot change. `GET /api/v1/goals` returns closed goals too, and `status` is `ACTIVE`, `ACHIEVED` or `ABANDONED`. Abandonment is not deletion and frees the subject for a new goal.
Two things the contract does not say. Find the answers in the backend before you design the controls, and do not guess.

1. Does `priority` 1 mean most important or least? Nothing in the contract says, and the consequence is not cosmetic: a control labelled the wrong way round inverts every student's intent and feeds the optimiser the opposite of what they meant. Read it in the backend — the planning module and the Core request mapping — and state what you found and where. If the backend is ambiguous too, stop and report rather than picking one.
2. What `PATCH /goals/{goalId}` does when you send only `priority`. It is required, so revising just the target date means sending a priority back. If the client sends a stale one it silently overwrites the student's choice. Check whether an omitted `targetDate` clears the date or leaves it, and make the screen send the values it actually read, not remembered ones. Record both answers; the first is a proposed finding for the backend if the contract stays silent.

Implementation
1. The setup gate — `/configurar`
Two steps, in this order, because the second is cheap to change and the first is the one the student has to think about. One screen with two sections, or two steps with visible progress — your call, justified.
Availability. A weekly routine: a weekday, a start and an end, from a date. Let the student add several windows, see what they have declared, and close one. Closing is `POST /availability/{windowId}/closure` with an `effectiveUntil`, and the window stays on record "keeping saying what it said for the period it covered. That is what a past plan was generated against" — so do not call it deleting, and do not let it look like deleting.
Goals. Pick a subject, optionally a target date, optionally a priority. The topic list of the chosen subject is shown as context. At most one active goal per subject: filter subjects that already have one rather than letting the student earn a 409, and still handle the 409 for the race.
Finishing. When there is at least one availability window and at least one active goal, `setupComplete` becomes true on the server. Call `SessionStore.refresh()` and let the chain route — do not navigate by hand, and do not decide locally that setup is complete. Say plainly what happens next, and do not promise a plan this task does not generate.
2. The account area — `/conta/disponibilidade` and `/conta/metas`
The same two things, kept current after setup. Reuse the setup components; do not write a second copy.
Goals here also get the two closing actions: abandonment (`POST /goals/{goalId}/abandonment`) and achievement (`POST /goals/{goalId}/achievement`), plus revision (`PATCH`). Show closed goals, because "what was abandoned is part of what the student has decided", and distinguish them by `status` without judging them: no red for abandoned, no green for achieved. The design-system rule holds — semantic colour describes the system, never the student. `recall-1`…`recall-4` and neutral tokens are what you have.
Both screens must survive a student with one window and one goal, and a student with twenty of each.
3. New components go through the design system
This task probably needs controls that do not exist yet: a weekly-window editor, a priority control, possibly a stepper. `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` is binding and its live source is the Design System artifact, so a component added only to `sinapse.css` puts the binding reference ahead of its source. FE-6 did that for links, correctly but silently.
So: build what you need with existing tokens, add no literal visual value, and list in the report every component and every rule you added, with what it is for — so the artifact can be brought level. If you need a token that does not exist, say why rather than inventing a literal.
